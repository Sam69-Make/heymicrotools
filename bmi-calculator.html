BMI formula & chart

Body mass index compares your weight with your height. It gives one number you can check against standard ranges to see whether your weight is likely to be a health concern.

Metric formula

BMI = weight (kg) ÷ height (m)²

Example: a person who weighs 70 kg and is 175 cm (1.75 m) tall. 1.75 × 1.75 = 3.0625, and 70 ÷ 3.0625 = 22.9.

Imperial formula

BMI = 703 × weight (lbs) ÷ height (in)²

Example: 154 lbs and 5 ft 9 in (69 in). 69 × 69 = 4,761, and 703 × 154 ÷ 4,761 = 22.7.

BMI categories for adults

Category BMI What it means
Underweight Below 18.5 Weight may be too low for your height.
Normal Weight 18.5 – 24.9 Weight is in the range linked to the lowest health risk.
Overweight 25 – 29.9 Weight is above the healthy range for your height.
Obese 30 and above Higher risk of weight-related health problems.

Keep in mind: BMI does not measure body fat directly, and it ignores muscle, bone density and where fat is stored on your body. Some health bodies also suggest lower cut-offs for people of South Asian and some other backgrounds, because health risks can rise at a lower BMI.

Use your result as a starting point for a conversation with a doctor, alongside other measures like waist size, blood pressure and blood tests.

Frequently asked questions

What is a healthy BMI?

For adults, the World Health Organization classes a BMI between 18.5 and 24.9 as normal weight. Below 18.5 is underweight, 25 to 29.9 is overweight, and 30 or above is obese. The weight that matches those numbers depends on your height, which is why the calculator shows a healthy weight range for you.

Is BMI accurate for athletes and muscular people?

Not always. BMI only uses height and weight, so it cannot tell muscle from fat. A muscular person can have a high BMI without carrying much body fat. In that case, measures like waist size or body fat percentage give a clearer picture.

Can I use this calculator for children or during pregnancy?

No. This calculator is meant for adults aged 18 and over. Children's BMI is compared against age and sex percentiles, and weight during pregnancy is assessed differently. Ask a doctor or paediatrician for guidance in those cases.
