How solar sizing works

Sizing a system is a short chain of simple steps. A rule of thumb to keep in mind: with about 5 peak sun hours, each kilowatt of panels makes roughly 120 units a month.

  1. 1

    Find your monthly use. Read the units (kWh) from a recent bill, or average several months. If you only know the bill amount, divide it by your price per unit. Without a bill, add up your appliances’ watts × hours.

  2. 2

    Turn it into a daily need. Divide monthly units by 30. For example, 450 kWh a month is 15 kWh a day.

  3. 3

    Divide by what 1 kW actually produces. Peak sun hours × 80% efficiency (for heat, dust, wiring and inverter losses). At 5 sun hours that is 5 × 0.8 = 4 kWh per kW per day. So 15 ÷ 4 = 3.75 kW.

  4. 4

    Round up to a standard size and count panels. 3.75 kW rounds up to 4 kW. With 550 W panels, 4,000 ÷ 550 = 7.3, so you need 8 panels, which makes a 4.4 kW array that generates about 528 units a month.

  5. 5

    Match the inverter. It is normally close to the array rating (4.4 kW of panels pairs with a 4 kW inverter). It must also cover the biggest load you run at once, which is why air conditioners matter in the appliance mode.

  6. 6

    Estimate payback. Divide the system cost by yearly savings. If a system costs 700,000 and saves 22,500 a month, payback is 700,000 ÷ (22,500 × 12) ≈ 2.6 years. We count savings only for units you actually use.

What changes the real answer

  • Shade and roof direction. A shaded or poorly angled roof can cut output noticeably, so a site survey matters.
  • Seasons. Output is lower in winter and monsoon months than the yearly average.
  • Net metering and batteries. Selling extra units back to the grid, or storing them in batteries, changes both cost and payback. This calculator assumes a grid-tied system without batteries.
  • Future use. If you plan to add an air conditioner or an electric vehicle, size up now.

Frequently asked questions

How many solar panels do I need for my home?

It depends on how many units you use and how much sun you get. As a rough guide, about 450 kWh a month needs a 4 kW system, which is eight 550 W panels. A good shortcut is a little under two 550 W panels per kilowatt. Use the calculator above for your own numbers.

How accurate is the payback period?

Treat it as a planning estimate. It divides the system cost by the yearly savings on your bill, using the price and cost you enter under Assumptions. Real results change with installation prices, your tariff, net metering rules, shading, and how your usage grows. Panels also lose a small amount of output each year. Compare the figure with a written quote from an installer.

What size inverter do I need, and do I need batteries?

The inverter is usually close to the size of the solar array, and it must also handle the largest load you run at the same time, such as an air conditioner starting up. Batteries are optional. You only need them if you want power during outages or at night. They add significant cost, so they lengthen the payback period.
