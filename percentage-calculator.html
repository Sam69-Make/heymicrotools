Formulas and examples

Every calculation above comes down to one short formula. Here is each one with a worked example you can check by hand.

X% of Y

X ÷ 100 × Y

Example: a jacket costs 80 and is 20% off. The discount is 20 ÷ 100 × 80 = 16, so you pay 80 − 16 = 64.

X is what percent of Y

X ÷ Y × 100

Example: you scored 32 out of 40. That is 32 ÷ 40 × 100 = 80%.

Percentage change from X to Y

(Y − X) ÷ X × 100

Example: rent goes from 50 to 65. The change is (65 − 50) ÷ 50 × 100 = +30%. A drop from 80 to 60 is (60 − 80) ÷ 80 × 100 = −25%.

Why +20% then −20% doesn’t get you back

Each percentage applies to a different starting number. Start at 100, add 20% and you have 120. Take 20% off that and you have 96, not 100. To undo a 25% drop you need a 33.3% rise. Keep this in mind whenever you compare changes in prices, investments or traffic.
